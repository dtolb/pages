The human behind the flow

What the conversation designer did

Conversation design is a craft, not a script — “a synthesis of voice UI, interaction, visual, motion and audio design, and UX writing,” practiced by someone who works as an architect: mapping what a person can do in the space and curating the flows and logic beneath it. Everything above is their output. Here’s how they think, what they produced, and where they sit in an AI-run contact center.

The spine · voice-first

The inbound call, step by step

One call: recognize, verify to a tier we can act on, capture intent, and either proceed or hand to a human — with the “just connect me to an agent” escape hatch as a designed path, not a dead end. Select a step to see what the caller hears and what the system does.

Platform layer

One thread across every channel

Conversation Orchestrator captures voice, RCS, and SMS into a single thread per customer (GROUP_BY_PROFILE). Memory carries identity and context; Intelligence analyzes every turn; TaskRouter routes the judgment moments to a human — who arrives already knowing the customer.

The tail · proactive follow-up

The transfer state machine

The magic lives in the weeks after the call. Each transfer walks a state machine; every transition fires a proactive, human-toned update. Step through it — watch the message the customer receives and the guardrails behind it.

branch

Rejected

Missing medallion signature → pair the problem with the next concrete action, re-send the form, loop back to Initiated. Repeat rejection → human.

branch

Reissue

Check exceeds the transit window → detect the stall proactively, start stop-payment + reissue, keep the customer posted. No action needed from them.

The intelligence layer

Operators watching every turn

Attached to the conversation, operators run per-message or at close — measuring sentiment, catching PII sent over text, extracting transfer status, and flagging the “5 minutes of yelling” moment before it happens.
